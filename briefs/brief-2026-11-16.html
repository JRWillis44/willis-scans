3) The Nexus Analysis

  • A Nvidia governance → Washington → Asia supply chain → EM equities. Treasury now openly shapes Nvidia's leadership (outside CEO, 10-day compliance deadline), while Musk and more than 165,000 signatories push to bring Huang back (Guardian). That uncertainty lands hardest on suppliers whose valuations rest on Nvidia's roadmap: Taiwan (TSMC is ~40% of the TAIEX) and Korea. Both carry large weights in EM indices, so the shock reaches EM funds through index weight, not through Chinese demand.
  • A Hawkish Fed + weak Japan/China/Europe → strong dollar → gold, crypto and EM under pressure. The dollar has been "more sensitive to the Fed terminal rate than energy prices" (JPMorgan via Dow Jones). Gold is under $3,900 and Bitcoin ~$64–65k, its lowest since mid-July. Japan's contraction makes a December BoJ hike harder (OIS still ~76% per Zanders) and keeps USD/JPY near 160.
  • A Gulf escalation ≠ oil spike, for now. Iranian strikes on a US host nation and Houthi gains at the Bab al-Mandeb would normally add a risk premium. Instead, Brent fell ~13% in a month because supply is returning (IEA: Q4 supply ~1.7 mb/d above demand per CNBC/Reuters; ~2 mb/d per Bloomberg) and China's refinery runs fell. The channel to watch is shipping insurance and freight, which KCM Trade says keep a floor under prices.
  • A Shenzhen trilateral → European security → defence and the euro. Washington floated Donbas-for-NATO with Kyiv, and Moscow's opening paper seeks NATO forces back to 1997 lines (NYT, per International Intrigue). If Thursday's three-leader meeting produces language on "European security architecture" without Europe present, expect pressure on EU fiscal plans (more defence spending) at a time when the euro is at a 19-month low and French fiscal worries weigh on it (Commerzbank).
