Polymarket gives Democrats ~72.5% and Kalshi ~71% for the Senate, and the House is widely expected to flip (Foretell). Options imply ~0.86% for today's session but ~1.85% through Friday's payrolls, so traders are paying for the week rather than the day (Saxo). Results start after ~6:00 PM CT. Watch three things: AMD guidance (~3:15 PM CT), the early Senate calls in PA, MI, TX, GA and NC (7–9 PM CT), and any US written reply to Qatar's text (we have high confidence these are the diagnostic indicators for Wednesday).

3) Nexus Analysis

  • Quiet strikes, then oil premium, then rates: F Brent +1.3–1.8% and the US 10y up 3 bp to 4.929%, with the 2y at 4.604% (CNBC). A The market is pricing a porous ceasefire in which limited strikes do not break talks. That is closer to the 1987–88 Tanker War, when Operation Praying Mantis-style US actions ran alongside UN diplomacy, than to a return to full war. The analog breaks if Iran retaliates against Gulf energy infrastructure rather than US assets.
  • AI capex, then memory prices, then device margins, then index concentration: F Apple's memory-cost warning comes after Alphabet's negative free cash flow and Oracle's $20B raise (Saxo). A The AI build-out is now a cost for parts of Big Tech, not only a revenue story. The Nasdaq-100's concentration means index-level drawdowns can continue even when memory makers' earnings rise.
  • Midterms, then Iran timing and fiscal policy: A A Democratic House reduces the scope for new fiscal expansion (SocGen: "more gridlocked") but does not constrain war powers in the short run. Washington's domestic gasoline-price constraint loosens after the vote. That supports our view that limited strikes continue regardless of the result.
  • China's capital-market props, then CNH and commodities: F The PBoC raised the FX deposit reserve ratio, the yuan weakened (CNH ~6.67), and copper fell 2.8% (Saxo). A Beijing is leaning against yuan strength and IPO supply at the same time. Together that is mildly negative for metals and EM FX, and supportive of A-shares.
  • Cyber as an infrastructure-war channel: F International Intrigue (Nov 3) lists the Binance $6B theft (attributed by the FBI to North Korea), hacked civil-alert apps in Ukraine and Russia, refrigeration sabotage in six EU states, and ransomware at Japan's GMO Internet that widened its 2031 bond spreads by 50 bp.
