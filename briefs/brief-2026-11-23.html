3) The Nexus Analysis

  • Havens sold → term premium → equity ceiling. A With stocks and bonds positively correlated again, a rally pushes yields up (2y +8 bp), which tightens conditions into a 91%-priced hike and caps the rally. Watch whether the 10y holds under 5.0% through the 2y auction at 12:00 PM CT. Treasury's public pressure on the Fed (Bessent, Sunday) is the counterweight, but the FOMC has not blinked so far.
  • Washington → corporate governance → AI risk premium. A The President lobbying Nvidia's board while US intelligence probes the Huang–MSS link puts a policy actor inside a mega-cap's governance. It adds a binary (reinstatement or not) to an SEC accounting probe. Either outcome keeps single-name volatility high, and the impact flows to the index through NVDA's weight.
  • Blockade attrition → Iranian escalation calculus → Gulf energy. F More than 80% of Iran's trade runs through Hormuz or its southern ports (Al Jazeera). A Economic pain plus a stalled US reply raises the odds that Iran shifts from tanker harassment to hitting US assets or Gulf energy sites ahead of Trump's "after the new year" clock. That is the chain that would reprice Brent from $90 to $100+. For now the market is fading it: Brent is down and Polymarket's ceasefire-holds odds rose to 70.5%.
  • Ukrainian long-range strikes → Russian refining/Druzhba → European diesel and politics. A Repeated Kaleykino hits threaten Druzhba flows to Hungary and Slovakia, which would sharpen EU splits on Ukraine. Refinery outages tighten Russian product exports and support diesel cracks even with crude soft.
  • Global growth softening → policy divergence → USD. F Eurozone services PMI came in at 51.2 vs 51.8 expected, UK services at 52.0 vs 53.0, and Australia's composite at 46.1, its weakest since Aug 2021 (FXNX/Forexlive). A The Fed is hiking while others' growth data roll over, which supports USD on the crosses (EUR/USD −0.19% to 1.0945) even as risk appetite returns.

If the rebound fades and the S&P closes under +1% with yields still up, the bear-flattening is doing the Fed's work and Friday's lows (6,910) come back into play.

  • Gulf hedge logic. Oil is pricing attrition, not escalation. The asymmetric signals are a CENTCOM statement on Chabahar, a Kharg tanker under way (TankerTrackers/satellite, not AIS), or any GCC energy-site impact. Any of those is a same-day repricing trigger; Iranian rhetoric alone is not.

  • Korea is the weak link. A won above ~1,360 with continued foreign selling would signal Seoul has to move from announcing to deploying the ₩10trn fund.
