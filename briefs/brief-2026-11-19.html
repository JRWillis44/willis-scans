3) The Nexus Analysis

  • IPO supply → AI rotation → Asian suppliers. F The tech giants are buying a combined $26B of ANT at the IPO price: AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC. In pre-market, GOOGL is up 3.9% and AMZN up 2.2%. In Asia, Tencent fell 6.8% after revenue grew only 8% and adjusted operating profit fell 6% while AI capex rose. Lenovo fell 7.4% even though AI revenue grew 79% (CNBC; Money & Banking; Sharecast). A The market is rewarding owners of AI models and distribution, and punishing capex spenders and component suppliers whose margins depend on Nvidia's pricing power. Taipei broker Li Fang-kuo makes this point explicitly: he warns that the AI industry's circular financing could tighten.
  • Washington's chip rule → Beijing's restraint → markets price the rule, not the summit. F Xi told Trump to "abandon Cold War thinking," and mainland chip-design stocks fell (Haiguang was down as much as 17% intraday) on the BIS remote-access rule (Yonhap Infomax; Sidley). A Warm summit rhetoric did not offset the regulation. Beijing's most likely next lever is a quiet lapse of the Nov 27 gallium and germanium suspension, not open retaliation.
  • Gulf escalation → oil, rates and the Fed's inflation problem. F Walmart's CEO says diesel costs are running higher than expected, offset so far by tariff refunds. The FOMC minutes flag AI capex as an inflation risk (CNBC). A Strikes on Iranian energy would hit an economy where the Fed is already hiking into above-target inflation. That combination is stagflationary, and the 1973–74 and 2022 episodes show it hurts both bonds and equities. This is why I keep the pessimistic pathway at 22% even though oil prices look calm.
  • Great-power text vs international courts. F On the same day, the ICJ found Myanmar responsible for genocide (the first ruling of its kind against a state), and the US, China and Russia agreed that the ICC "must not abuse" its jurisdiction (Reuters). A The international legal order is fragmenting: rulings are getting bolder while the big powers align on immunity. The near-term market impact is low, but it raises sanctions and enforcement risk for exposures in Myanmar and in Israel-adjacent defence.

8) Strategic Insights (PM / policy analyst)

  • Separate AI owners from AI suppliers. Today's tape pays model and distribution owners (hyperscalers with ANT stakes) and sells leveraged hardware supply chains. Expect Taiwan's new short-selling curbs to produce a relief bounce, as such measures usually do, but they do not fix margin deleveraging. Wait for foreign selling to slow before calling a bottom.
  • The oil market is short convexity. A strike option that includes energy sites is openly reported, while Brent sits near 2½-month lows and physical buyers quietly lock in term supply (QatarEnergy). Hedges built on upside optionality cost less than the size of the event justifies. Watch war-risk premiums and VLCC rates rather than headlines.
  • The Fed is set. The tails are what move. At 91.5% for a 25 bp hike, the information is in the 50 bp tail (6.5–9.9%) and in January pricing. November payrolls (Dec 4) are the last big data point before the meeting.
  • Policy: the US signed a text with Moscow and Beijing that limits the ICC, which gives Putin a diplomatic win and Beijing a "three great powers" frame. Expect European and Japanese pushback. Tokyo (Takaichi) has already said the trilateral "won't jeopardise Japan's interests."
