  • US export rules → TSMC curbs → Asian hardware. F Sources tell Reuters that TSMC has tightened curbs on Chinese AI-chip designers and suspended some advanced orders as new US rules close a manufacturing loophole. TrendForce reports that TSMC and Samsung paused some China consumer-chip shipments, including 16nm orders (Taipei Times/Reuters; TrendForce). A The Asian chain is losing Chinese volume at the same moment US AI capex is being repriced, so the drop compounds. That explains why Taiwan (−17.5% on the week) fell far more than the US (S&P about −5%).
  • Equity losses → margin calls → selling of "hedges." F Gold fell with equities, and a Reuters-quoted analyst describes "a bit of forced liquidation going on across the market" (T&I News/Reuters). A In de-leveraging phases, correlations converge. Today Treasuries, the yen and the Swiss franc are working as havens; gold is not.
  • AI capex → expensive credit → valuations. F AI-chip debt is clearing at about 230 bp over the high-grade index average (8.26% vs 5.95%) (The Edge/Bloomberg). A When capex funding costs rise, the market applies a higher discount to AI earnings. The next AI-financing deals will show whether this cost is rising further.
  • Hormuz tempo vs supply surplus → oil skew. F The EIA says Middle East chokepoint shipments rose 19% q/q in Q3 and regional output reached 23.1 mb/d in October. The IEA sees a 1.7 mb/d surplus in Q4, widening to 3.8 mb/d in 2027 (WSJ via Yahoo). Disagreement: straits.live, citing IMF PortWatch, logged only 2 transits on Nov 15 and calls the strait "effectively closed," while the EIA and Reuters say flows are near pre-war. The likely cause is dark/STS traffic that AIS-based counts miss. A Prices reflect the surplus; the tail is a strike on energy infrastructure.
  • Kremlin pressure during peace talks → European risk premium. F Trump told European leaders that Putin "is tired… ready for a deal." The same night Russia launched a ten-hour strike and missiles landed in Poland near the Koalicja 2026 exercise. Russia's MoD says it hit military, energy and transport targets and has not commented on Poland (CNN; BBC). A Moscow is probably signalling deterrence against the post-deal force (moderate confidence), and EUR is feeling the pressure.
  • Key assumptions check. (1) The sell-off is a de-rating, not an earnings collapse. Supported: NVDA beat, and no TSMC/Foxconn output cuts have been announced (Taiwan News). (2) The Fed will not pivot because of equities. Supported: 92–93% hike odds and Paulson and Musalem both hawkish. Load-bearing: this breaks if credit seizes. (3) Gulf kinetic risk has been deferred to 2027. Weakly supported: Israel says it may act alone, and the IRGC rejects "neither war nor peace" (Press TV via ANI).
  • ACH-lite on why AI is selling off. H1 is valuation and financing de-rating. H2 is a policy shock (BIS rule, TSMC curbs, the Nvidia CEO exit). H3 is a rates shock. Falling yields while stocks fall are inconsistent with H3. Taiwan underperforming the US fits H2. Wide SpaceX pricing and ANT giving back gains fit H1.
