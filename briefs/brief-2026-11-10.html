3) The Nexus Analysis

A Oil down, rates up: the two main drivers now pull in opposite directions. Falling crude should ease the inflation outlook (Brent is ~23% below its Oct 30 peak), but October's CPI captures the earlier spike. The Fed is signalling that it will hike anyway to protect credibility, which Waller said openly. The chain to watch: hot core CPI → 50 bp talk → 2-year and 10-year yields up (2y 4.62%, 10y ~4.89% before the print per CNBC) → pressure on long-duration tech, which also carries the Nvidia overhang → weaker auction demand at noon CT.

A The Gulf's risk premium has left the futures price but not the water. Physical signals are mixed. Hormuz transits are still ~65% below pre-war levels per UKMTO (Gulf News), yet Reuters reports Dated Brent weakening and OPEC output rising. Iran's public line (Araghchi: the war ends only through "acceptance of the Islamic Republic's conditions"; Rezaei: "FOLD YOUR HAND") contrasts with Trump's "positive momentum" (Arab News/AFP). Source split: US and Gulf-market outlets frame this as de-escalation; Iranian state media (Tasnim) frame it as the IRGC enforcing a "wall of fire" on ships using US-protected routes. The asymmetry favours an upside oil surprise if the status quo breaks.

A Iran's pressure is moving into Europe through hybrid means. France formally blamed the IRGC's Shahid Kaveh cyber unit for sabotaging refrigeration at ~700 supermarket sites, summoned Iran's ambassador and will seek EU sanctions (France Diplomatie; AP). With the Mildenhall threat, this suggests Tehran is answering the naval blockade with cyber and base threats in Europe rather than only in the Gulf. This channel is under-priced by markets.
